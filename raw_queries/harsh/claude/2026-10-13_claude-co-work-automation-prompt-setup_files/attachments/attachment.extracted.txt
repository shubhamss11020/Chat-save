CREATIVE DIRECTOR ASSESSMENT
- Discussed: a small contractor sales team (about four people) quotes slowly because material lists from plans must be re-typed into their point-of-sale system. Quote time is a week to ten days. AskCruz explained a "company brain" that connects email, calls and the ERP so AI knows the business, then walked through plan -> material list -> own product codes -> stock and supplier lead-time check -> draft quote -> human approval.
- Most quotable moment: the prospect explaining that the quicker you get a number back, the more likely the job converts, and that word gets around in a small market. Also: the salesperson must still be the final say on price.
- What it shows about AskCruz: we do not sell generic AI. We make AI context-rich by plugging it into the customer's own systems, with a human as the last check.
- Problem a viewer recognises: "Our quotes take too long and the customer goes elsewhere."

COUNCIL OF FOUR
- Strategist: Quote speed is a concrete, measurable outcome and ties straight to the "context-rich AI / company brain" positioning. Strong. Avoid pricing talk.
- Storyteller: Clear tension (waiting customer vs. slow re-typing), clear payoff (quote out in under 2 days, human still approves). The "tribal knowledge when someone leaves" beat is a good second hook but would make it two ideas.
- Skeptical Buyer: Cares because it is about winning jobs, not "AI". Wants proof it is not a black box: the human-approval line answers that. Would doubt vague hype, so no big claims.
- Social Editor: Hook as a direct question about the viewer's own customer stops the scroll better than a statistic.
Alternatives considered: (a) "Onboarding by asking the AI instead of a 30-minute call", (b) "Generic AI vs context-rich AI". Both lost: (a) is internal-facing, (b) is too abstract for a cold viewer.
DECISION: One idea - "The first quote in the inbox usually wins, so make yours the fastest." Hook: "How long does your customer wait for a quote?"
