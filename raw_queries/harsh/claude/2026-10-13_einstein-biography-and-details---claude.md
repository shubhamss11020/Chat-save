Albert Einstein (1879–1955) was a German-born theoretical physicist, and he is best known for the theory of relativity and for the equation E = mc².

**Early life**
- He was born on March 14, 1879, in Ulm, Germany, and grew up in Munich.
- He disliked rigid, rote-learning schooling but taught himself geometry and calculus as a teenager.
- He graduated from the Swiss Federal Polytechnic in Zurich in 1900 and became a Swiss citizen in 1901.
- From 1902 he worked as a patent clerk in Bern, which left him time to think about physics.

**The "miracle year" (1905)**
He published four groundbreaking papers while working at the patent office:
1. **The photoelectric effect.** He proposed that light comes in discrete packets (quanta), which helped found quantum theory. This earned him the 1921 Nobel Prize in Physics.
2. **Brownian motion.** He explained the random movement of particles in fluid, which gave strong evidence that atoms and molecules exist.
3. **Special relativity.** He showed that the laws of physics are the same for all non-accelerating observers and that the speed of light is constant. This leads to time dilation and length contraction.
4. **Mass-energy equivalence.** This is the famous E = mc².

**General relativity (1915)**
- He extended relativity to gravity, describing it as the curvature of spacetime caused by mass and energy.
- In 1919, Arthur Eddington's solar eclipse observations confirmed that starlight bends around the Sun, as the theory predicted. This made Einstein world famous.
- The theory later predicted black holes, gravitational waves (detected directly in 2015), and the expansion of the universe.

**Career and later life**
- He held professorships in Zurich, Prague, and Berlin, and became director of the Kaiser Wilhelm Institute for Physics in 1914.
- He left Germany in 1933 after the Nazis came to power and settled at the Institute for Advanced Study in Princeton, New Jersey. He became a U.S. citizen in 1940.
- In 1939 he signed a letter to President Roosevelt warning that Germany might develop atomic weapons. This helped spur the Manhattan Project, though he did not work on it. He later expressed regret and became an advocate for peace and nuclear disarmament.
- He spent his last decades searching for a unified field theory combining gravity and electromagnetism, without success.
- He was skeptical of the probabilistic interpretation of quantum mechanics. His remark that "God does not play dice" reflects his debates with Niels Bohr.

**Personal life and views**
- He married Mileva Marić in 1903, and they had children. They divorced in 1919, and he then married his cousin Elsa Löwenthal.
- He played the violin and loved sailing.
- He supported civil rights, pacifism, and Zionism, and he declined an offer to become Israel's president in 1952.

**Death and legacy**
- He died on April 18, 1955, in Princeton, from a ruptured abdominal aortic aneurysm.
- His work underpins modern cosmology, GPS timing corrections, and much of modern physics, and his name has become a byword for genius.
